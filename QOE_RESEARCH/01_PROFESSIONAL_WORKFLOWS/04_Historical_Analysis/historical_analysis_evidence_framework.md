# Quality of Earnings (QoE) Research Programme
## Workstream 04 — Historical Financial Analysis
### Deliverable 7: Historical Financial Analysis Evidence Framework

This matrix establishes the non-negotiable evidentiary standards, primary source requirements, investigative inquiry protocols, and acceptance/rejection criteria governing the interpretation of historical financial performance in a professional transaction advisory engagement.

---

### Master Analytical Evidence Matrix

| Procedure ID & Analysis Focus | Financial Movement Examined | Initial Analyst Hypothesis | Mandatory Primary Evidence Required | Corroborating Operational Records | Key Management Inquiries | Evidentiary Limitations & Traps | Conditions for Accepting Explanation | Conditions Requiring Escalation / Downstream Referral | Downstream Workstream Referral |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HFA-01: Top-Line Revenue Growth** | Revenue grew >10% YoY across historical periods. | Growth is a combination of organic volume and contractual price adjustments; check for unearned pull-forwards. | `EVD-005`: Transactional sales registers, customer invoices with SKU quantities and unit pricing. | Customer Master Service Agreements (MSAs), signed purchase orders, shipping bills of lading. | "What proportion of growth was driven by price increases vs unit volume? Which specific customer contracts accounted for the expansion?" | Inconsistent units of measure across SKU families; year-end bill-and-hold orders included in sales. | 100% of revenue growth reconciled to customer invoices, with physical proof of delivery dated within the fiscal period. | Revenue growth driven by price increases without volume, or concentrated in a single customer account. | **REF-001:** Refer to **WS06** (Customer Concentration & Revenue Sustainability). |
| **HFA-02: Price-Volume-Mix Decomposition** | Revenue growth outpaced volume, or gross margin shifted across product lines. | Top-line growth was inflated by commodity price surcharges rather than commercial market share gains. | `EVD-005`: SKU-level sales registers; `EVD-007`: Raw material commodity supplier price indices. | Product item master files, catalog list price sheets, sales rep discount authorization logs. | "Were price increases implemented across the board or targeted to specific customer tiers? Did customers push back or demand rebates?" | Transactional data aggregated at high product family level, hiding SKU-level mix shifts inside the price bucket. | Mathematical PVM reconciliation ties out to penny; realized price increases match customer price change notices. | Realized price increases failed to cover raw material cost inflation; volume contracted in core profitable lines. | **REF-002:** Refer to **WS06** (Commercial Pricing Power & Margin Durability). |
| **HFA-03: Gross Margin Compression** | Gross margin percentage contracted by >100 bps over the historical period. | Direct input cost inflation (materials, energy, freight) outpaced customer price adjustments due to contract lag. | `EVD-007`: Vendor raw material invoices, steel/chemical spot market indices; `EVD-008`: Stock status reports. | Bill of Materials (BOM) standard cost cards, freight carrier rate tables, scrap logs. | "What was the contractual time lag between vendor cost increases and customer price surcharges? Are commodity contracts hedged?" | Management blaming 'global inflation' without showing invoice-level vendor cost increases; unposted inventory write-downs. | Vendor invoices show verifiable raw material price increases matching the timing and dollar impact of the margin drop. | Margin compression is structural; customer contracts prohibit price pass-throughs; competitors did not suffer margin drops. | **REF-003:** Refer to **WS05** (EBITDA Normalization for Temporary Cost Spikes). |
| **HFA-04: Direct Labor Overtime Surge** | Direct manufacturing labor costs grew significantly faster than revenue. | Shop floor labor productivity deteriorated, or labor shortages forced high overtime premiums. | `EVD-010`: Shop floor electronic timecard extracts, gross-to-net payroll processor registers. | Machinist recruitment agency invoices, plant shift logs, union collective bargaining contracts. | "Why did overtime hours surge from 6% to 15%? What was the turnover rate among skilled shop floor machinists?" | Contract agency labor expensed in SG&A consulting rather than direct COGS labor; labor capitalized into ending WIP. | Timecard logs corroborate that overtime hours were paid at 1.5x/2.0x rates to fulfill firm customer delivery deadlines. | Overtime is permanent due to structural local labor shortages; wage rates must be adjusted upward permanently. | **REF-004:** Refer to **WS05** (Overtime Normalization & Run-Rate Labor Costing). |
| **HFA-05: Factory Overhead Over-Absorption** | Gross margin expanded sharply during periods of high manufacturing production. | Plant over-produced inventory to absorb fixed overhead, artificially lowering COGS and boosting EBITDA. | `EVD-016`: Factory overhead spending ledgers, standard cost BOM cards, plant production run tickets. | Finished goods warehouse capacity utilization logs, physical inventory count variance reconciliations. | "What was the plant capacity utilization by month? Were machine lines run specifically to build safety stock?" | Standard absorption rates changed mid-year without disclosing policy revisions; SG&A corporate overhead allocated to plant. | Actual incurred overhead matches standard absorption; physical inventory turnover supports production volume. | Plant produced excess inventory that remains unsold; fixed overhead was capitalized into balance sheet assets. | **REF-005:** Refer to **WS05** (Inventory Over-Absorption Normalization Haircut). |
| **HFA-06: SG&A Operating Leverage Stalling** | Operating expenses grew faster than revenue, resulting in negative operating leverage (DOL < 1.0). | Management added corporate overhead (executives, software, leases) in anticipation of growth that failed to materialize. | `EVD-002`: Monthly close packs; `EVD-010`: Employee census; `EVD-011`: Facility lease agreements. | Vendor consulting contracts, software license agreements (SaaS seats), sales commission plans. | "Which corporate headcount additions were discretionary? What proportion of SG&A is fixed vs variable?" | One-time M&A sell-side advisory fees, litigation settlements, or owner personal perks buried in recurring opex. | Line-by-line contract review verifies recurring nature of salaries, rent, and software licenses. | G&A contains non-recurring transaction expenses, or standalone corporate costs will be stranded post-acquisition. | **REF-006:** Refer to **WS05** (Standalone Cost Structure & Non-Recurring Add-Backs). |
| **HFA-07: Executive Compensation Below Market** | Management salaries remained flat or declined despite rapid revenue growth. | Selling shareholders under-paid themselves in salary, taking cash out via shareholder dividends to boost reported EBITDA. | `EVD-010`: Executive employment agreements, W-2 annual wage statements; `EVD-013`: Corporate tax returns. | Independent third-party executive compensation benchmarks (Mercer, Radford, Pearl Meyer). | "What were the total annual cash distributions taken by selling owners? Will selling executives remain post-close?" | Owner personal expenses (auto leases, club memberships, family travel) run through corporate SG&A accounts. | Payroll records corroborate base salary; shareholder distributions verified via tax return Schedule K-1 / M-2. | Current executive compensation is below fair market value; a replacement C-suite will require higher post-close salaries. | **REF-007:** Refer to **WS05** (Pro-Forma Market Compensation Normalization Adjustment). |
| **HFA-08: Quarter-End / Year-End Hockey Stick** | Revenue and EBITDA spiked dramatically in the final month of the quarter or fiscal year. | Management accelerated sales cut-offs, offered heavy year-end discounts, or executed bill-and-hold shipments. | `EVD-005`: Daily sales registers for the last 15 days of the period; warehouse bills of lading. | Customer purchase orders with stated shipping terms, subsequent bank deposit slips clearing in January. | "Why did December represent 10% of revenue and 16% of EBITDA? Were extended payment terms or return rights granted?" | Verbal assurances from management that 'customers always buy in December' without third-party delivery proofs. | Subsequent cash collections in January prove customer acceptance; bills of lading confirm shipment occurred before close. | Goods shipped after period end (FOB Destination cut-off violation); customer granted extended return privileges. | **REF-008:** Refer to **WS05** (EBITDA Cut-Off Reversal) and **WS06** (Revenue Cut-Off Diligence). |
| **HFA-09: Working Capital Velocity Drag (DSO / DIO)** | Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) or Days Inventory (DIO) expanded significantly. | Cash is trapped in unpaid customer receivables or slow-moving inventory buffer stock. | `EVD-006`: Detailed AR aging as-of balance sheet date; `EVD-008`: Perpetual inventory aging by SKU. | Customer dispute logs, credit memo registers, warehouse stock movement reports (dead stock listings). | "Why did DSO expand by 6 days? Are large customers delaying payment due to product quality disputes?" | Management claiming uncollected receivables are '100% good' despite being >90 days overdue. | Customers confirm payment schedules; aged inventory verified as active catalog items with ongoing sales velocity. | Overdue receivables represent uncollectible disputed balances; inventory contains obsolete, unsellable SKUs. | **REF-009:** Refer to **WS07** (Working Capital Peg & Bad Debt / Obsolescence Haircut). |
| **HFA-10: Cash Conversion Ratio Deterioration** | Operating Cash Flow (OCF) / EBITDA dropped below 60%. | Paper EBITDA profits are being absorbed by working capital expansion, CapEx, or aggressive revenue recognition. | `EVD-009`: Direct bank statements, 4-column proof of cash; `EVD-011`: CapEx invoices. | Bank debt covenant compliance certificates, cash flow reconciliation workbooks. | "Why did Operating Cash Flow drop 10% while EBITDA grew? How much of capital expenditure was strictly maintenance?" | Cash flow statement prepared using inconsistent non-cash working capital adjustments; tax payments excluded. | Proof of cash corroborates cash collections; cash conversion bridge mathematically ties EBITDA to bank cash. | Business requires high working capital reinvestment to support growth; debt service capacity is constrained. | **REF-010:** Refer to **WS07** (Cash Flow Debt Capacity & Debt-Like Item Assessment). |
