# QoE Simulator Research Programme — Part C
# Master Project Charter, Evidence Framework, and Research Protocol

**Project Title:** The Realism of a Simulated Quality of Earnings Engagement  
**Programme Phase:** Part C — Empirical Engagement Realism and Simulation Specification  
**Governing Mandate:** Discover, with evidence, what separates a convincing simulated QoE engagement from an artificial recreation of one.  
**Core Axiom:** *Research reality first, identify artificiality second, and derive simulator requirements third.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Programme Architecture

In Parts A and B of the Quality of Earnings (QoE) Simulator Research Programme, we completed two foundational phases:
1. **Part A (Empirical Data Acquisition & Architectural Foundation):** Acquired and structured 942.6 MB of physical transaction-level datasets across diverse ERPs (AkzoNobel SAP ERP, Czech Bank, SF Public Compensation, UCI Retail, Olist Marketplace, SEC EDGAR FSDS, Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy Examiner records) and established the core parameter catalog.
2. **Part B (Professional Due Diligence Workstream Analysis):** Exhaustively researched and codified the nine core financial due diligence workstreams (WS01 Data Intake through WS09 Final Reporting & Transaction Closing Deliverables), audited against strict Quality Gates G1–G7, supplemented by operational manuals, business model guides, and cross-industry frameworks.

While Parts A and B defined the **mechanics, analytical tools, and technical calculations** of Financial Due Diligence (FDD), **Part C addresses the realism of the simulation environment itself**.

A simulation can execute mathematically correct EBITDA reconciliations, working capital peg formulas, and revenue waterfall bridges while remaining completely artificial, sterile, and unconvincing. Real M&A financial due diligence does not occur in an aseptic spreadsheet environment where data is neatly structured, management is instantly cooperative, transactions are logically isolated, and findings follow a linear sequence from Day 1 to Day 30.

Real engagements are characterized by:
- Fragmented, messy, and delayed information flow;
- Political, cognitive, and defensive frictions from client management;
- Conflicting records and timing anomalies arising from real-world business operations;
- Evolving deal hypotheses, shifting transaction timelines, and aggressive buyer/seller negotiation pressures;
- Iterative, non-linear investigative discovery where an initial conclusion is upended by a footnote in a board pack or an obscure journal entry.

The objective of Part C is to conduct an evidence-led research project across **eight sequential modules** to discover the true mechanics of engagement reality, systematically catalogue how simulations fail into artificiality, and author the definitive **QoE Simulation Realism Specification**.

## 2. The Central Research Questions

### 2.1 The Overarching Central Research Question
> **What makes a simulated Quality of Earnings engagement look simulated rather than like a genuine professional engagement, and what must be reproduced to make the simulation convincingly realistic?**

### 2.2 The Five Supporting Research Questions
1. **Business & Financial Reality:** *What characteristics distinguish real operating companies and their financial information from artificially generated companies?* (Module 02)
2. **Documentary & Accounting Realism:** *What difficulties, uncertainties, and irregularities occur in genuine financial records, period-end cut-offs, and supporting schedules during actual QoE engagements?* (Module 03)
3. **Client & Human Dynamics:** *How do real clients, management teams, sellers, lenders, and QoE professionals behave, communicate, stall, defend, and interact under deal pressure?* (Modules 01, 04, 06)
4. **Investigative Credibility:** *What makes a financial investigation, its hypothesis testing, its evidentiary burden, and its final reporting conclusions credible and defensible?* (Modules 05, 07)
5. **Artificiality Testing & Validation:** *How can we systematically test, benchmark, and validate whether a simulated engagement is convincingly realistic and free of artificial "tells" or gameable patterns?* (Module 08)

## 4. The 7-Step Research Protocol (Activities A through G)

To prevent Part C from degenerating into intuitive software speculation, every module must strictly execute seven sequential research activities:

```
[A. Establish Reality] ➔ [B. Gather Evidence] ➔ [C. Understand Variation] ➔ 
[D. Identify Artificiality] ➔ [E. Establish Consequences] ➔ [F. Define Requirements] ➔ [G. Validate]
```

1. **Activity A — Establish Reality:** Map the actual operational, behavioral, and technical reality of the topic as it occurs in genuine institutional transactions.
2. **Activity B — Gather Evidence:** Anchor every finding in empirical evidence according to our 6-level hierarchy (professional standards, Big 4 manuals, transaction records, practitioner accounts).
3. **Activity C — Understand Variation:** Analyze how this reality shifts across transaction types (corporate carve-out vs. founder sale vs. sponsor secondary), deal sizes (lower middle market \$10M–\$50M EV vs. upper middle market \$100M–\$500M EV), and business models (Distribution, Manufacturing, SaaS).
4. **Activity D — Identify Artificiality:** Catalogue the specific, granular failure modes where current business simulations, case studies, or synthetic datasets fail and reveal themselves as artificial.
5. **Activity E — Establish Consequences:** Analyze the cognitive and educational damage of each artificiality failure mode (e.g., teaching trainees to rely on clean trial balances or expect immediate truthful answers).
6. **Activity F — Define Requirements:** Formulate precise, functional simulation specifications that a simulation engine must implement to eliminate the artificiality.
7. **Activity G — Validate:** Pressure-test the proposed requirements against concrete worked scenarios and verified historical transaction case studies.

## 5. The Evidence Hierarchy (Levels 1 through 6)

Every factual assertion, behavioral parameter, and simulation requirement must be tied to a documented evidence classification:

| Evidence Level | Source Classification | Description & Acceptable Citations |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1** | **Professional Standards & Authoritative Guidance** | AICPA Statements on Standards for Consulting Services (CS Section 100), Statements on Quality Management (SQMS), ICAEW Technical Releases (TECH 04/20 CFF, TECH 09/13 FDD), FASB ASC / IFRS authoritative accounting literature. |
| **Level 2** | **Published Firm Methodologies & Institutional Guidance** | Methodologies, white papers, and thought leadership from Big 4 (PwC, EY, Deloitte, KPMG) and Tier-1 M&A advisory firms (Alvarez & Marsal, FTI Consulting, Houlihan Lokey, Lincoln International, Baird). |
| **Level 3** | **Real-World Transaction Documents & Public Case Records** | Authenticated historical transaction documents, court filings, Delaware Court of Chancery post-closing dispute decisions, SEC EDGAR exhibits, Lehman Bankruptcy Examiner Reports, public M&A filings. |
| **Level 4** | **Practitioner Accounts & Documented Experience** | Transcripts, memoirs, practitioner interviews, professional PE/FDD forums, published post-mortems by experienced dealmakers, corporate development heads, and diligence partners. |
| **Level 5** | **Academic & Empirical Research** | Peer-reviewed academic literature on financial accounting manipulation, synthetic data fidelity, economic agent-based modeling, behavioral economics, and simulation realism. |
| **Level 6** | **Explicit Proposed Assumptions** | Engineering and design assumptions formulated specifically for the simulator where direct Level 1–5 evidence is unavailable or proprietary. Must be explicitly flagged as Level 6. |

*Strict Rule of Evidence:* Marketing claims from financial software vendors, student case competitions, and unverified AI hallucinations are strictly excluded from Levels 1–5.

## 6. The Seven Quality Gates (G1 through G7)

A module cannot be closed or considered certified until it has undergone an independent evaluation against seven quality gates:

- **Gate G1 (Understanding):** Does the analysis explain the genuine real-world activity with absolute technical and operational precision?
- **Gate G2 (Evidence):** Is every material claim supported by identifiable citations classified under Levels 1 through 5?
- **Gate G3 (Operational Detail):** Does the module reveal the granular "how" (day-to-day interactions, workpaper mechanics, communication friction) rather than high-level generalities?
- **Gate G4 (Variation):** Does the module account for structural differences across deal types, target maturity levels, and business models?
- **Gate G5 (Artificiality):** Does the module provide an exhaustive catalogue of failure modes and synthetic "tells"?
- **Gate G6 (Requirements):** Are the resulting simulator specifications unambiguous, actionable, and mathematically/logistically defensible?
- **Gate G7 (Validation):** Are the requirements validated through end-to-end worked scenarios demonstrating real-world operational causality?

*Certification Ratings:* Each gate receives **PASS**, **PARTIAL**, or **FAIL** with comprehensive explanatory reasoning. Any gate receiving PARTIAL or FAIL requires immediate remediation before advancing.

---

## 7. Operational Rules & Boundaries

1. **No Premature Implementation:** This phase is exclusively research, empirical analysis, and functional specification. We will not write simulator application code, develop user interfaces, or generate production synthetic datasets until Part C specifications are completed and audited.
2. **Reality Before Synthetic Data:** We study the real-world operational and behavioral reality first. We do not generate mock financial files until we have defined the operational reality that generates them.
3. **Traceability:** Every simulation requirement must point backward to an identified artificiality tell, which points backward to an empirical real-world behavior, which points backward to Level 1–5 evidence.
