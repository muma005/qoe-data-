## Research Assignment: Understanding the Human Side of Real QoE and Financial Due Diligence Engagements

### 1. Research objective

Conduct a rigorous investigation into the people involved in real Quality of Earnings (QoE) and Financial Due Diligence (FDD) engagements, their responsibilities, incentives, behaviour, interactions and influence on the engagement.

The purpose is to understand how human behaviour affects:

- Access to company information.
- The quality and completeness of information received.
- The explanations provided by management.
- The investigation of unusual financial results.
- The identification and resolution of issues.
- Communication and coordination.
- Professional judgment.
- The reliability of conclusions.
- The final assessment communicated to the client or transaction stakeholders.

We need to understand the difference between a technically correct financial analysis and an actual professional engagement involving people with different responsibilities, priorities, interests and levels of knowledge.

**This is a research module, not an attempt to create autonomous human agents or automate professional judgment.**

Do not develop the simulator, generate synthetic conversations or implement behavioural models during this research.

7. What happens if the explanation is incomplete?
8. What happens if the explanation conflicts with accounting records?

### 6. Research area D â€” Cooperation, resistance and information withholding

Investigate the different ways participants respond to scrutiny during diligence.

Research documented examples and professional observations of:

**Cooperative behaviour**
- Prompt responses.
- Voluntary disclosure of relevant information.
- Clear explanations.
- Identification of known limitations.
- Assistance in tracing transactions.
- Willingness to correct mistakes.

**Ordinary operational constraints**
- Staff unavailable.
- Documents held by external accountants.
- Incomplete historical records.
- Limited finance-team capacity.
- Difficulties extracting information from systems.
- Competing transaction and operational deadlines.

**Defensive or resistant behaviour**
- Disagreement with the diligence team's interpretation.
- Reluctance to provide sensitive information.
- Delayed responses.
- Repeated explanations without supporting evidence.
- Disputes about the relevance of requests.
- Resistance to proposed adjustments.

**Potentially concerning behaviour**
- Contradictory explanations.
- Selective information provision.
- Unsupported assertions.
- Changes in explanations after challenge.
- Inconsistent representations across participants.
- Evidence suggesting deliberate misclassification or concealment.

For each behaviour establish:
- Its observable form.
- Plausible causes.
- What can and cannot be inferred from it.
- How a professional team typically responds.
- Whether escalation may be appropriate.
- The potential consequences for the engagement.

Important: a late response is not proof of dishonesty. A disagreement is not proof of concealment. A difficult management team is not necessarily a dishonest management team.
